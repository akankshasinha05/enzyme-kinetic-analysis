1. THE BIOCHEMICAL THEORY (IN EASY LANGUAGE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is an Enzyme?
An enzyme is a protein that acts as a biological catalyst. In living cells, essential
chemical reactions that would naturally take hundreds or thousands of years happen in fractions
of a second because enzymes stabilize transition states and lower the activation energy.

How an Enzyme Reaction Works:
    Enzyme (E) + Substrate (S) <---> [Enzyme-Substrate Complex (ES)] ---> Enzyme (E) + Product (P)

Key Concepts & Formulas:
1. Initial Velocity (v0):
   - The initial rate of product formation at the very beginning of the reaction, before substrate
     is significantly depleted and before any reverse reaction can occur.
   - Measured in micromoles per minute (µM / min).

2. Michaelis-Menten Equation:
   - When substrate concentration [S] is very low, velocity increases linearly with [S].
   - When substrate concentration [S] is very high, all active sites of the enzyme become saturated,
     and velocity approaches a maximum ceiling called Vmax.
   - Formula:
         v0 = (Vmax * [S]) / (Km + [S])

3. What is Vmax?
   - Maximum Velocity: The theoretical upper speed limit of the enzyme when 100% of enzyme
     molecules are occupied by substrate.

4. What is Km (Michaelis Constant)?
   - The substrate concentration [S] required for the enzyme to reach EXACTLY HALF of its maximum speed (½ Vmax).
   - Low Km = The enzyme has VERY HIGH AFFINITY for its substrate (binds tightly and works fast even at low amounts).
   - High Km = The enzyme has LOW AFFINITY for its substrate (needs high substrate concentrations to work efficiently).

5. Turnover Number (k_cat):
   - Formula: k_cat = Vmax / [E]0 (where [E]0 is the total enzyme concentration).
   - Unit: per second (s⁻¹).
   - Meaning: How many substrate molecules one single enzyme molecule can convert into product per second!

6. Catalytic Efficiency (k_cat / Km):
   - The ultimate measure of an enzyme's evolutionary perfection. It accounts for both how fast
     it turns over substrate (k_cat) and how well it grabs substrate out of solution (Km).
   - Theoretical maximum (diffusion limit) is around 10^8 to 10^9 M⁻¹ s⁻¹.

7. Why Linear Plots (Lineweaver-Burk & Hanes-Woolf) Exist:
   - Before modern computers and Python non-linear solvers, scientists linearized the curve:
     * Lineweaver-Burk Double Reciprocal:
           1 / v0 = (Km / Vmax) * (1 / [S]) + (1 / Vmax)
       Plotted with y = 1/v0 and x = 1/[S].
       The y-intercept gives 1/Vmax.
       The negative x-intercept gives -1/Km.
     * Hanes-Woolf:
           [S] / v0 = (1 / Vmax) * [S] + (Km / Vmax)
       Less vulnerable than Lineweaver-Burk to distortion caused by small errors at low substrate amounts.

8. Enzyme Inhibition (How Drugs and Regulators Work):
   - Competitive Inhibition:
     * The inhibitor molecule mimics the substrate and competes directly for the active site.
     * Effect: Km increases (apparent affinity drops), but if you add enough substrate, it can outcompete
       the inhibitor, so Vmax remains UNCHANGED!
     * On Lineweaver-Burk: Lines intersect exactly at the y-axis (same 1/Vmax).
   - Non-Competitive Inhibition:
     * The inhibitor binds elsewhere (an allosteric site) and distorts the enzyme's catalytic machinery.
     * Effect: Adding more substrate cannot rescue the distorted enzymes, so Vmax DECREASES. But the
       uninhibited enzymes still have normal binding affinity, so Km remains UNCHANGED!
     * On Lineweaver-Burk: Lines intersect exactly at the negative x-axis (same -1/Km).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. LINE-BY-LINE / BLOCK-BY-BLOCK CODE EXPLANATION (analyze_kinetics.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Lines 1-13: Imports & Windows UTF-8 Configuration
-> Configures sys.stdout to handle UTF-8 symbols (like ±, ², and s⁻¹) gracefully on Windows terminals.
-> Imports NumPy (math & arrays), Pandas (tables), SciPy curve_fit (nonlinear regression), and Matplotlib (charts).

Lines 15-20: michaelis_menten(s, v_max, km)
    def michaelis_menten(s, v_max, km):
        return (v_max * s) / (km + s)
-> Implements the exact Michaelis-Menten formula. Given substrate concentration s and parameters v_max and km,
   it computes the expected reaction speed.

Lines 22-31: load_kinetics_data(filepath)
-> Reads the raw CSV file using pd.read_csv().
-> Identifies all replicate columns ('replicate_1_velocity', etc.).
-> Computes the average velocity ('mean_velocity') and experimental standard deviation ('std_velocity') for each substrate concentration.

Lines 33-50: fit_nonlinear_model(s_data, v_data)
-> Uses SciPy's 'curve_fit()' function to perform true non-linear least-squares optimization.
-> Finds the exact Vmax and Km values that minimize the squared distance between observed points and the theoretical curve.
-> Computes the standard error (uncertainty) of both parameters from the covariance matrix (pcov).
-> Computes the Coefficient of Determination (R²) to measure statistical goodness of fit (close to 1.0000 = exceptional fit).

Lines 52-70: fit_lineweaver_burk(s_data, v_data)
-> Inverts the data: inv_s = 1/[S] and inv_v = 1/v0.
-> Fits a straight line (y = m*x + c) using np.polyfit().
-> Back-calculates Vmax from 1/intercept, and Km from slope * Vmax.
-> Computes R² in reciprocal space.

Lines 72-88: fit_hanes_woolf(s_data, v_data)
-> Computes s_over_v = [S] / v0.
-> Fits a straight line against [S].
-> Back-calculates Vmax from 1/slope, and Km from intercept * Vmax.

Lines 90-117: plot_michaelis_menten(...)
-> Generates 300 evenly spaced points from 0 to max [S] to draw a smooth, continuous saturation curve.
-> Plots the experimental data points with error bars (mean ± standard deviation).
-> Draws horizontal reference lines for Vmax and ½ Vmax, and a vertical line for Km.
-> Saves the 300 DPI chart to 'figures/michaelis_menten_curve.png'.

Lines 119-149: plot_lineweaver_burk(...)
-> Plots 1/v against 1/[S].
-> Extrapolates the regression line backward across the y-axis to the negative x-axis.
-> Highlights and labels the critical intercepts: (0, 1/Vmax) and (-1/Km, 0).
-> Saves the graphic to 'figures/lineweaver_burk_plot.png'.

Lines 151-209: analyze_inhibition(filepath)
-> Loads the multi-treatment inhibition dataset:
   1. Control (uninhibited)
   2. Competitive Inhibitor (+ Inorganic Phosphate Pi)
   3. Non-Competitive Inhibitor (+ EDTA)
-> Loops through each condition and fits non-linear models.
-> Creates a dual-panel figure:
   * Left panel: Hyperbolic saturation curves showing shift in Km vs drop in Vmax.
   * Right panel: Lineweaver-Burk diagnostic showing intersection points.
-> Saves the comparative table to 'results/inhibition_kinetics_comparison.csv' and the figure to 'figures/inhibition_diagnostic_plot.png'.

Lines 211-262: main()
-> Master function that drives the entire pipeline:
   1. Loads the Alkaline Phosphatase kinetics data.
   2. Fits both non-linear and linear models.
   3. Computes k_cat and catalytic efficiency (k_cat/Km).
   4. Prints formatted statistics to the terminal.
   5. Generates the Michaelis-Menten and Lineweaver-Burk figures.
   6. Runs the inhibition mode diagnostic analysis.
   7. Exports clean CSV summary tables to 'results/'.
